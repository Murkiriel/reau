Frequently asked questions

A complete guide to installing, using and testing REau. Guide of 19 Oct 2026, reference: REau 0.1.0.

⚠️ REau is a personal, independent project, still in testing. It is not made by Royal Enfield and has no connection to it, to Google or to Waze. Every rider installs and uses it at their own risk. On the road, eyes on the road: touch the phone only while the motorcycle is stopped.

The screenshots in this guide come from the app’s Portuguese interface; the text uses the English labels of the app (Settings → App language lets you switch REau to English).

1. What REau is

What does REau do?

It brings the Android Auto of your own phone, with Waze (or another Android Auto navigation app), to the motorcycle’s round dash. No cable, no car head unit and no extra device. The phone can stay in your pocket with the screen off.

REau does not draw a map or calculate a route: it only repeats on the dash what Android Auto draws, cropped and scaled to the dash’s screen.

How does it work, in one sentence?

REau “pretends” to be a car’s head unit inside your own phone. Android Auto sends it the video; REau crops and scales that video to the dash’s round screen and streams it over the dash’s Wi-Fi.

Why the name REau?

It is read “real”. It joins RE (Royal Enfield) with AU (Android Auto).

Which motorcycles does it work with?

With Royal Enfield motorcycles that have the round Tripper Dash TFT display (for example, the Himalayan 450’s). Compatibility was verified with dash firmware 13.10; other versions may work but have not been verified. The Bike data screen shows your dash’s firmware.

Does it work on iPhone?

No. REau is an Android-only app and depends on Android Auto, which does not exist on iPhone (the iPhone equivalent is CarPlay). A similar project for iPhone would be a different app, and a much harder road: it needs a paid annual developer license, and there is a risk of Apple rejecting or banning the app. For example, there is an iPhone port of NorthStar called Cairn, made by someone else.

Which phones and Android versions work?

Requirements: Android 10 or newer, with Android Auto installed and up to date. REau was tested with Android Auto 17.8 and 17.9. From Android Auto 17.4 on, the head unit server is turned on as described in section 4.

Is it easier than Royal Enfield’s official app?

Usage reports indicate that it is easier to connect and to set the route than in the official app. The main difference is that you use full Waze (or another Android Auto app), not the official app’s map. These are individual reports: the Terms of use warn that the picture arrives a few seconds late and can freeze.

Does it replace Waze’s voice?

No. Waze’s voice keeps coming out of the phone or the helmet intercom, as always.

Can I use REau together with the official Royal Enfield app or another dash app?

Not at the same time. Two apps connected to the dash fight over the same connection and the result is unpredictable. Close the other app (and stop its streaming) before turning REau on. If that happens, REau warns “Another app has the dash” and tries again on its own once the dash is free.

Does REau change or write anything on the dash?

REau does not write settings or firmware to the dash: it sends the picture and some display data (maneuver, music, caller…) and only reads what the dash reports (the Bike data screen). Even so, using REau can make the dash behave unexpectedly, freeze or show wrong information, and can affect warranties; the Terms of use explain these risks. Everyone uses it at their own risk.

Is it paid? Does it have an account or ads?

It is free software (AGPL-3.0 license), with no account, no ads and no data collection. The source code address is on the Credits screen, at the end of “REau and your data”.

Where do I get the APK? How do I know which version I have?

From the Releases page of the project’s repository (its address is on the app’s Credits screen). The app version is in the footer of the home screen. Official (release) APKs are called 0.1.0; test (debug) builds add the build date and time, such as 0.1.0-dev+26.10.19-12.46. Quote that text when reporting a problem.

There are two APKs. Which one should I install?

Lite is the recommended one. Full is only worth it if you want REau to do the Android Auto server step by itself and switch the crop when the apps menu opens.

Which file do I download on the Releases page?

On the Releases page, each version carries one .apk file per edition, and the name tells you which is which: REau-0.1.0-lite.apk is Lite (the recommended one) and REau-0.1.0-completa.apk is Full (“Completa” is Portuguese for Full). The 0.1.0 is the version and changes with each Release. Download only the file of the edition you chose; when in doubt, download the one with “Lite” in its name. Ignore the “Source code” items (zip and tar.gz): they are the source code and cannot be installed.

Full uses the Accessibility service and can make banking apps refuse to open (see the question about banking apps, further down). If you use a banking app, download the file with “Lite” in its name.

Why is Lite the recommended one?

Because it does not ask for the Accessibility service: it gets no broad control of the phone and does not clash with apps that refuse to work while Accessibility is on. The price is a manual step every time: starting the head unit server in Android Auto (section 4).

Can Full’s Accessibility get in the way of my banking apps?

It can. Some apps, mainly banking apps, treat third-party Accessibility as a risk and may refuse to open or to sign in. There are reports of the block continuing even after uninstalling REau. There is a report of a government-benefits app that stopped opening after installing Full; it only showed up when testing on a phone where those apps were installed.

  • If you use a banking app, use Lite.
  • If you already installed Full and an app refused to open: turn REau’s Accessibility off in Android’s settings and switch to Lite. If the app keeps refusing, contact its support.
  • REau asks for confirmation (“Allow Accessibility?”) before opening the Accessibility page, precisely to warn about this.

Can I install both editions?

You can install both, but only one can be on at a time: the Android Auto server takes one session at a time and the two would fight over the dash. If both are installed, REau warns “Another REau is installed”: turn the other one off, wait about a minute and the connection happens on its own.

What happened to the old “with accessibility” and “without accessibility” APKs?

On 10 Oct both were the same app (com.reau.app), one with and one without the Accessibility service. Today’s “without accessibility” is Lite (com.reau.app.lite), a separate app, and the old APK no longer needs updating: if you want to stay without Accessibility, install Lite, and if you want Full, install the current version over the old one.

How do I update to a new version?

Download the new APK from the same link and install it over the old one, without uninstalling. That keeps your settings and the Wi-Fi of the bike already learned. Uninstalling erases everything, including logs and the permissions already granted. If Android refuses to install over the old one (it happens when the new APK is signed with a different key, as may happen with the first official Release), you will have to uninstall the old one first, and the settings are lost.

Is there a way to avoid starting the server by hand without using Accessibility?

No. From Android Auto 17.4 on, Google closed the automatic path: either you start the server by hand (Lite) or Accessibility starts it for you (Full).

3. Installation and permissions

Play Protect blocked the installation. Now what?

That is expected on Full. Play Protect blocks apps from outside the Play Store that declare an accessibility service (“App blocked to protect your device”). To install:

  1. Open the Play Store → profile picture → Play Protect → gear.
  2. Pause app scanning.
  3. Install the APK again.
  4. If you like, turn Play Protect back on afterwards.

The block does not always happen: sometimes the install goes straight through. Lite normally installs without this problem. Pausing Play Protect removes a protection from the phone: install only the official APK, from the project’s Releases page, and turn Play Protect back on right after.

Turning on accessibility shows “Restricted setting” or “App access denied”. What do I do? (Full)

It is a protection in Android 13 and newer for apps installed from a file (it also applies to an APK downloaded outside the Play Store). Follow this order, because the option to allow it only appears after the first attempt:

  1. Try to turn it on first. In Settings → Accessibility, tap “REau: Android Auto server”. “Restricted setting” or “App access denied” appears. Close the warning.
  2. Allow restricted settings. In Settings → Apps → REau → ⋮ menu (top corner) → “Allow restricted settings”. The phone asks for your fingerprint or PIN. Shortcut: on REau’s Permissions screen, the “Allow restricted settings” button opens that same screen.
  3. Turn accessibility on. Go back to Accessibility, turn on “Use REau: Android Auto server” and tap Allow.

If you go straight to step 2, the “Allow restricted settings” item does not show in the menu. And if your Android doesn’t even show the ⋮ or lets you turn it on at the first try, it doesn’t require this step: go straight to 3.

Where is REau’s accessibility on my phone brand?

  • Samsung: Settings → Accessibility → the installed apps list → “REau: Android Auto server”. That is the only difference: the rest is the same.
  • Motorola, Pixel and others: Settings → Accessibility → the downloaded apps list → “REau: Android Auto server”.

What does REau’s Accessibility do? Does it touch my other apps?

It was made for only two things: turn the Android Auto server on and off (it opens Android Auto’s settings, taps ⋮ and the “Start head unit server” item) and know which Android Auto screen is open (apps menu, Waze, Maps) to switch the crop. It only “listens” to Android Auto, Google Maps and the call notifications of WhatsApp (including Business), Telegram and Signal (and only with the calls switch on). Android’s warning says “full control of your device”: that is the standard text for any accessibility service.

  • It only taps the item with the exact name of the server; it never taps “Quit developer mode”.
  • It does nothing while the phone is locked and does not touch other apps.
  • If you force stop REau, Android turns its Accessibility off: open REau and tap Turn on so it recovers (or turn it back on in Accessibility).

According to the author, Google has been tightening the rules for installing outside the store, even for developers: a rule started on 30 Sep 2026 in Brazil and a few other countries and is meant to reach the whole world by 2027. How this will affect independent projects like REau is not yet known; this section will be updated.

4. Developer mode and the Android Auto server

I already turned on my phone’s developer mode. Isn’t that enough?

No. This is the most common mix-up: they are two different things. REau needs Android Auto’s developer mode, which lives inside Android Auto’s own settings. The phone’s developer mode is not needed.

How do I turn on Android Auto’s developer mode?

You do this once:

  1. In REau, tap “Open Android Auto settings” (or open it from the phone: on Samsung, Android Auto has no icon and lives inside Settings).
  2. Scroll to the bottom and tap about 10 times on Version (in the version and permissions information).
  3. Confirm the question about enabling developer settings (OK) (Android Auto’s warning reminds you that these settings are for developers and that you use them entirely at your own responsibility).

After that, Android Auto’s ⋮ menu (top corner) has three new items: Developer settings, Start head unit server and Quit developer mode.

Do not tap “Quit developer mode”: you would have to redo the 10 taps.

If Android Auto’s ⋮ menu doesn’t show “Start head unit server”, developer mode isn’t on yet: redo the taps on “Version”.

What is the “head unit server”?

It is the “front door” Android Auto opens for an external head unit. Since Android Auto 17.4, this is the only way for an app like REau to connect to it. The server should turn off when the session ends (it sometimes takes a few minutes), when the phone restarts or when Android Auto updates.

How do I turn the server on? (Lite: by hand every time)

  1. Turn the bike on, open REau and tap Turn on. The “Head unit server is off” warning appears.
  2. Tap “Open Android Auto settings”.
  3. In the ⋮ menu, tap “Start head unit server”.
  4. Go back to REau. It connects by itself (or tap Try again).

From the second time on, developer mode is already on: only step 3 is needed each time. It is a good idea to turn REau on near the bike, because it starts hunting for the dash right away.

How does REau turn the server on by itself? (Full)

With REau’s Accessibility turned on and the “Turn on the server automatically” switch on (it is on by default, in Settings), when you tap Turn on REau opens Android Auto’s menu, taps “Start head unit server” and comes back. When you turn the projection off, it turns the server off the same way. If Settings says “To turn the server on automatically, enable REau in Accessibility”, accessibility is not on yet.

What is that black screen “Turning on the Android Auto server…”?

It is accessibility at work: under that cover, REau opens Android Auto’s settings, taps ⋮ and starts the server. It goes away on its own in a few seconds; Cancel interrupts it. The phone must be unlocked: with it locked, REau says “Unlock the phone and tap Try again”.

Why isn’t the server left on all the time?

It could be turned on once and left until the phone restarts, but the author preferred “turn everything off, then everything on”: for security (the server listens for connections while it is on) and battery (it is not yet known how much it drains idle). Leaving it always on may become an option in the future.

Can Android Auto be started fully automatically?

Up to Android Auto 17.3 it could; from 17.4 on, Google closed that path. The only known one today is the developer server. The downside: being a developer feature, Google may change or remove it in an update.

Does developer mode break Android Auto in my car?

No, in the test that was run: with developer mode on in the phone, a car’s Android Auto worked normally (19 Oct 2026).

5. First connection to the bike and Wi-Fi

What happens the first time I tap Turn on?

  1. With the bike on, REau looks for the dash’s Wi-Fi network, which starts with RE_ (“Looking for the dash”).
  2. Android asks “Connect to device”. Tap Connect. This only happens the first time; REau waits up to 2 minutes for your tap.
  3. The phone leaves your home Wi-Fi and joins the bike’s network, as with other dash apps.
  4. REau connects to the dash and to Android Auto, and the picture appears.

The home screen shows both sides, ANDROID AUTO and DASH. When both turn green (“Active” and “Ready”), REau is Streaming.

If the phone stays on the bike’s Wi-Fi, does Waze lose the internet?

No. The bike’s network has no internet, but mobile data keeps working for Waze. REau uses the bike’s network only to talk to the dash.

REau can’t find the bike’s Wi-Fi. What do I do?

  • Check that the bike’s key is on and the dash is lit.
  • Allow Location for REau: without it Android doesn’t hand over the list of networks. (REau doesn’t read your position for this.)
  • Android only lets an app list networks while REau is open on screen: turn the projection on with REau open, near the bike.
  • In Bike data, type the dash’s full name in the “Full Wi-Fi name” field (as it shows in the phone’s Wi-Fi list, for example RE_AB12_345678). The dash only accepts the full name.

For privacy the name shows masked (RE_••••30); the Show button displays the whole name.

I changed motorcycle, or there is more than one dash nearby.

In Bike data, tap Forget. It deletes the saved dash name, everything the dash has already reported and the network suggested to Android; then REau searches again. Check the name before continuing.

I use a VPN. Is that a problem?

Yes. No app can talk to the Royal dash with a VPN running through it, and REau warns with the card “A VPN is holding REau back”. There are two ways:

  • Turn the VPN off while you use REau.
  • Exclude REau from the VPN (“split tunneling” in the VPN app) and fully disconnect and reconnect the VPN for the exception to apply.

The dash refuses the connection, or REau says “Another app has the dash”.

  • Another app has the dash: another dash app (such as the official one) is streaming and holds the phone’s port 2002. Stop its streaming; REau tries again on its own.
  • The dash refuses the connection even though everything is right: the dash may have frozen. Turn the motorcycle off with the key, wait about 30 seconds and turn it back on; REau keeps trying on its own.

I turned on the “network server” and REau doesn’t look for the dash.

That is how it works: with “Turn on the network server” on (section 12), REau does not look for the dash, because the idea is to run on another device. Turn that switch off to go back to using the bike.

The picture dropped on the way. What if I turn the bike off?

If the dash’s Wi-Fi disappears, REau notices within a few seconds, warns and reconnects by itself, without freezing. When the Wi-Fi returns (for example, when you turn the key on again), the dash resumes streaming within a few seconds, with no wait. The dash’s Wi-Fi dropping at the end of a ride means the key was turned off, and it is normal.

6. Everyday use

What is the routine for going out on the bike?

  1. Turn the bike on.
  2. Open REau and tap Turn on (on Lite, start the Android Auto server first: section 4).
  3. Set the route in Waze (see the next question).
  4. Put the phone away. You can switch the screen off and put it in your pocket.

To finish, tap Turn off in the app or in the notification.

I turned it off and on quickly and it didn’t connect.

After Turn off, Android Auto takes up to about 1 minute to close the previous session and, meanwhile, accepts the connection without answering. REau shows “Android Auto is closing the previous session” and tries again by itself. To skip the wait, in Android Auto’s settings stop and start the head unit server again (⋮ → “Stop head unit server”, then “Start head unit server”).

How do I set the destination in Waze?

With the bike stopped, in one of these ways:

  • In the Waze app on the phone: open it, set the route and it shows on the dash.
  • By voice: tap Android Auto’s microphone button and say the destination.
  • Through REau’s “Android Auto screen” (see the next question).

Google Maps opened instead of Waze, or the dash shows the apps menu. How do I switch?

Android Auto opens the last navigation app used. And if Waze gets closed on the phone (for example, during a long stop), Android Auto returns to the apps menu. To switch:

  1. On the home screen, tap “Android Auto screen”.
  2. Tap and drag on the picture to use Android Auto, as if it were a car screen. The dash keeps receiving the picture.
  3. Open the apps menu and choose Waze.

While riding, and only with the bike in a safe condition, you can use the joystick (apps menu button) to find the Waze icon and open it. When stopped, the app is easier. Opening Waze straight from the dash depends on Android Auto and how it manages apps; REau cannot force that. After using Waze once, it tends to open directly.

When should I use the phone and when the joystick?

  • Stopped: use the phone, which is much easier.
  • Riding: use the joystick, only for the essentials (zoom, music, answering a call).

Do settings apply right away or do I have to turn it off and on?

The LIVE tag marks what applies right away, with the projection on: language, theme, phone status, album art, real route, weather, using the location, answering calls, logs and the joystick. On the Android Auto screen, fit, crop, position and the FPS text also apply right away. DPI and margins apply right away but restart Android Auto (the picture freezes for a few seconds). The rest applies the next time you turn on.

What happens if I lock the phone screen?

On the dash, nothing changes: the projection continues normally. What stops is the preview inside the app (Android Auto screen), because there is no way to use it with the phone locked. That also explains the “glitchy” picture some people saw in the preview: with the phone in use, it generates two different videos, one for the dash and one for the app’s screen.

Can I close REau from the recent apps?

The projection runs as a service, with the pinned notification, and keeps going even if the app is swiped away from recents by accident. If something won’t turn on, it sometimes helps to clear REau from recents and open it again.

7. Picture on the dash: fit, crop and mask

Why does the map on the dash look cropped?

Android Auto always draws at 800×480, and the dash screen receives 526×300, without the two top corners, which sit behind the ring. REau crops and scales the picture to fit in the most legible way possible.

How do I adjust the picture?

In the “Android Auto screen” (shortcut on the home screen). It shows the preview and lets you adjust:

  • Mode: Fill the dome (Android Auto’s picture as large as possible inside the dash area; the corners may end up behind the round edge), Whole image (smaller, nothing hidden) or Center crop (takes only a middle part and enlarges it; the crop’s size and place are adjustable).
  • Move the picture horizontally and vertically, one by one.
  • Android Auto DPI and margins (see below).
  • App fit (Waze, Maps, Spotify…) and Menu fit (app grid). Reset to default undoes everything.

What are “App fit” and “Menu fit”?

They are two different framings, because the apps menu and Waze put things in different places. App fit applies to Waze, Maps, Spotify and the like; Menu fit applies to Android Auto’s app grid.

On Full, REau notices which screen is open and switches the fit by itself. On Lite, the app fit always applies: it works, but the menu ends up worse cropped or small.

What is the “Mask”?

It is the button at the top of the Android Auto screen. When on, it darkens on the phone the part that doesn’t reach the dash’s round screen and shows where the ring is. It is for adjusting the fit while seeing the result. To see Android Auto without the darkening, turn the mask off.

What is the “Android Auto DPI” for?

It controls the size of the text and buttons Android Auto draws. The default is 180; larger makes text and buttons bigger. Changing the DPI restarts the Android Auto session for a few seconds.

What are the “Margins”?

Android Auto draws its screen in a smaller area, centered in the 800×480 frame, with black around it. The margin says how much is taken off the width (or height), split evenly between both sides, so the picture already comes out in the shape the dash needs. It also restarts Android Auto for a few seconds.

My Android Auto app bar ended up on the wrong side (covering the map).

Android Auto’s side bar (clock, apps, microphone) can be on the left or on the right. You have two options:

  • Change the bar’s side in Android Auto’s settings. A good choice is to keep the bar on the side opposite the dash’s bubble.
  • Adjust the crop in REau for the side where the bar is. You can also show Android Auto’s full screen: it is a matter of taste.

What is the “Wallpaper”?

It is a still picture sent to the dash, without Android Auto, to test the dash and measure the screen (“Send wallpaper”, on the home screen). You can choose your own picture in Settings → Wallpaper (the ideal size is 526×300); “Reset to default” brings back the test grid. For normal use you don’t need it.

What can I test in Waze to improve performance?

Waze draws the map, and “heavier” maps cost more. Worth testing:

  • Turn off “Auto zoom” (Waze → Settings → Map display). With it, Waze zooms out at high speed and, on the small screen, shows “the whole city”. After the fixed video profiles (section 9), auto zoom does run on the dash, but many people still prefer it off, because of the screen size.
  • 2D map instead of 3D, because 3D costs a bit more. (Not measured yet; it is a good thing to test and report.)

Can I watch a movie or Netflix on the dash?

It makes no sense: the dash receives 2 to 4 frames per second and has limited Wi-Fi. It is made for a map, not for video.

How does the joystick work with REau?

Can I change what each button does?

Yes, in Settings → Dash joystick. Tap the control and then the action. There are 10 options: nothing, previous item, next item, confirm, back, apps menu, play/pause, next song, previous song and Gemini. The change applies right away, even with the projection on. “Reset to default” undoes everything. The “locked” controls in map mode belong to the dash itself and cannot be changed.

Tip: the joystick takes a little while to get the hang of; try it while stopped before you ride.

How do I answer calls with the joystick?

With the “Answer calls” switch on (it is on by default; it asks for the phone permissions). It works in both modes of the dash and for carrier calls and voice-app calls such as WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal, through Android Auto:

  • If the helmet intercom answers first (after about 7.5 s of ringing), a tap on the center hangs up the call that was already answered.
  • During a call the dash keeps left and right for itself; REau doesn’t receive those taps.

Can I use Gemini (the voice assistant)?

Yes, with the “Gemini” switch (on by default; it asks for the microphone). Choose the “Gemini” action for one of the controls on the joystick page (by default it is Down in media control). The voice goes through the helmet’s Bluetooth headset, or through the phone’s microphone if there is no headset. REau does not store the audio.

A blue border showed up around the map. What is it?

It is Android Auto’s “focus”: it appears when you use the joystick and, in Android Auto, normally only goes away with a tap on the screen. REau removes the focus by itself about 1 minute after the last joystick use.

The joystick doesn’t respond in map mode.

The dash only passes the joystick on in map mode when its maneuver bubble shows something. With no route, REau shows the arrival pin in the bubble precisely for that. If it stops responding, turn the projection off and on and, if it continues, open an issue in the project’s repository with the log (section 15).

Can I remove the maneuver ring (the white circle on the left)?

It can’t be changed. The maneuver ring is native to the dash’s system: REau only sends which instructions to show (the maneuver icon and the distance). It also can’t be turned off without losing function: you lose the joystick in map mode. Most people prefer it with the maneuvers on.

9. Performance and smoothness

Why isn’t the picture on the dash smoother?

It is a limitation of the dash itself and its Wi-Fi, not of the phone. Royal Enfield’s official app also has only two fixed video profiles: 4 frames per second at 200 kbps near a maneuver and 2 frames per second at 100 kbps while cruising, switching by distance to the maneuver, not by real network conditions. REau sends more than that, but the dash has a limit.

What are REau’s video profiles?

  • Constant bitrate and a keyframe every 1 second (a broken picture lasts that long at most).
  • Profile by maneuver (on): within 1 km of the next maneuver, A; farther away, B.
  • Profile by speed (on): up to about 55 km/h (34 mph), A; from about 65 km/h (40 mph), B. The speed is estimated from the route Android Auto reports, without REau turning on GPS, so it uses no extra battery.
  • If either asks for B, B wins. The profiles only decide while a route is active.

Can I raise the frames per second or the bitrate?

No: the profile values are fixed, to avoid headaches. What the tests showed:

  • At 30 frames per second, the dash dropped and did not reconnect; at 16, it held, but it isn’t worth it.
  • A 200 kbps cap doesn’t work for Waze: the quality gets bad. 400 kbps is stable.
  • At high speed the problem is not the app or the FPS, it is the bitrate: the dash’s Wi-Fi can’t take more, and a map that changes a lot (accelerating in the city) hits the ceiling and stalls.
  • More frames at the same bitrate don’t make the picture sharper; compressing too much makes it horrible. REau can’t compact all the information Android Auto sends beyond that.

On the highway, at high speed, the picture freezes or lags. Is that normal?

It was REau’s issue number 1 and is still being followed. Reports:

  • City and low speed: works well.
  • On a highway at 120 km/h (75 mph), in the first-day build, there were big jumps and stalls with Waze alerts (speed camera, bump, hazard, pothole) and heavy traffic.
  • On the 17 Oct build (fixed profiles, 400 kbps), there is a report of riding at 130–140 km/h with a delay described as very small. These are individual reports: the Terms of use warn that the picture arrives a few seconds late.
  • The cause has not been fully isolated: the delay may come from Waze, from Android Auto’s queue or from the dash itself, which doesn’t report how far behind it is.

If it happens to you, send the log (section 15), with the approximate speed and what Waze was showing.

What is the “PICTURE STUCK” / “No new picture” tag?

With the bike moving on a route and Android Auto sending no new picture for 15 seconds, the dash shows the PICTURE STUCK tag at the bottom, and the notification and home screen say “No new picture”. Before, the dash repeated the last picture and looked alive. The tag goes away when the picture returns. Stopped or with no route there is no warning, because then Android Auto really is quiet.

What is “Android Auto has no picture”?

It shows when the connection opens but Android Auto sends no frame for 10 seconds (Android Auto’s own encoder is stuck) and the dash is connected. Tap Try again; if it persists, follow what the warning says (force stop Android Auto (Settings → Apps → Android Auto → Force stop) and then turn REau off and on; on Lite, with Android Auto 17.4 or newer, also check that the head unit server is on). If the dash is black, this is it.

What is the red “fps” and “kbps” text over the video?

It is the “FPS and bitrate on the video” test switch (Android Auto screen), which writes the frames per second and the bitrate of the last 5 s over the video. It is for testing only; it sits over the map, so leave it off day to day.

10. Battery and heat

Does REau use a lot of battery?

REau itself uses little. What uses most is Android Auto with Waze (GPS, map, data) and the video stream. References: an hour running with the phone in a pocket; in a test between city and highway, with the phone charging, it “didn’t get hot”; a whole night of 7 h of projection stayed stable in memory, with no leak.

Why exclude REau from battery optimization?

With the screen off, Android cuts the network and pauses background apps. Outside optimization, REau keeps streaming with the phone in your pocket. Use the “Exclude REau from optimization” button on the home screen.

“Battery saver is on” appeared.

With battery saver on and the screen off, Android cuts the network and location for background apps. REau keeps going thanks to its pinned notification, but if the dash or Android Auto drops, turn battery saver off.

What if the phone gets hot in the sun?

REau reads the phone’s temperature and lowers the frames per second by itself when it heats up.

11. What else shows on the dash

Does the dash show the next-turn arrow?

Yes. The dash’s native bubble, the circle on the left (the maneuver ring), shows the maneuver arrow and the distance Waze reports. It is more legible than the reduced map. REau uses the motorcycle’s original model, which makes everything more integrated with the dash.

Do the time left, arrival time and total km show?

Waze shows the time left, arrival time and remaining km inside the picture itself. The dash shows, in its top bar, the route’s total km (it comes from Android Auto’s protocol 1.7, now REau’s default) and the arrival time on its card. The experimental “Real route on the dash” switch (below) also sends the dash the time left and the turn after the next one.

Do the song name and the caller’s name show on the dash?

Yes, on the dash’s own cards, with the “Now playing” and “Who’s calling” switches (Settings → On the dash; they are on by default). The data comes from Android Auto itself, without asking for notification or contacts access. Album art is another switch (off by default). Riders with an intercom can answer with the joystick, and the contact’s name shows on the dash. The call card is the motorcycle’s original model: it is a white card that covers the bike’s position on the map a little, but the maneuver ring keeps guiding you.

Does the dash show the phone’s battery, GPS and signal?

Yes, with the “Phone status” switch (on by default): the dash shows the phone’s real battery, GPS and signal. When off, it shows fixed values that say nothing about the phone.

Can I see the weather on the dash?

Yes, it is optional and off by default. With the “Weather on the dash” switch (and “Use location” on), REau queries the Open-Meteo service when it opens and every 15 minutes and sends the dash the temperature and the weather icon. The Weather shortcut shows now, 8 hours, 8 days, a 14-day chart, wind, UV, air quality, sun and moon. It is REau’s only use of the internet. Note: the temperature in the dash’s top bar comes from the motorcycle’s own thermometer.

What is the “Real route on the dash”?

An experimental switch (off by default) that sends the dash the time left on the route and the turn after the next one, and the “no signal” icon when Waze reroutes with no internet.

What is the “Bike data” screen?

A screen that only shows what the dash reports (it sends nothing to it; the Wi-Fi fields are at the top): known dash and Wi-Fi, last reading, range (how far it can still go on the fuel it has, as the dash itself counts), firmware version, variant, dash settings (temperature, distance, theme, consumption, clock, language), ABS and the low-fuel warning. “Not reported yet” means the dash hasn’t sent that item yet: REau asks when it connects and every 15 minutes.

How does the light and dark theme work?

REau’s theme is the day and night signal it sends to Android Auto. By the sun (default) is light from sunrise to sunset and dark at night; the times come from the phone’s position if “Use location” is on, or from the time zone (around 6 AM and 6 PM). Light and Dark fix the theme. Waze and other apps follow only if their theme is set to automatic.

Can I fix the dash brightness in day mode?

Not through REau. The dash only has two theme modes, automatic (which alternates by the light sensor) and dark. Fixing the brightness would require fooling the sensor or modifying the dash’s firmware, which is a lot of work and outside REau’s scope.

Can I clear or change the dash’s service reminders?

Not through REau: it does not change the dash’s settings or reminders. That is not something the app does.

Can I use apps other than Waze?

Yes. Android Auto lets you open other apps (Google Maps, Spotify and the like) and REau shows whatever is on screen. The project’s focus, however, is Waze.

12. Extras: network server and language

What is “Turn on the network server” (Android Auto in the browser)?

An optional feature (off by default) made to mirror Android Auto on an old head unit or a computer. With the projection running, REau opens a server on the local network; another device with a browser, on the same network, opens the address, enters the 4-digit PIN and sees Android Auto, with optional touch and sound.

  • Don’t use it on a ride: while on, REau does not look for the dash.
  • The PIN changes every time; after 5 wrong attempts the device waits 5 minutes. At most 2 browsers.
  • Traffic is not encrypted and doesn’t go through the internet: don’t turn it on in networks shared with strangers.
  • It turns itself off when the projection ends. The touch switch (“Allow touch from the browser”) is off by default.

Which languages does REau speak?

Portuguese and English. The app follows the phone’s language and there is a selector (Automatic / Português / English) in Settings; English is the default for anyone using another language. On Android 13 or newer, changing the language no longer flickers the screen.

What are the terms of use that show up on first open?

The app asks you to accept the terms of use (in Portuguese and English) on first open. The “I accept” button only appears after they have been read to the end (or tap “Scroll to the end”). They remain available at the end of the “REau and your data” screen, together with the Credits.

14. Privacy and data

Does REau use the internet or send my data anywhere?

No, with one optional exception. It only talks to Android Auto, inside the phone itself, and to the dash, over the bike’s Wi-Fi, which has no internet. What leaves the phone goes only to the dash: the picture and, if you turn the switches on, the song name, the caller and the phone status. The only trip to the internet is Weather on the dash (Open-Meteo), off by default. With the network server on, what leaves also reaches the devices on your local network.

Does REau know where I am?

Not to find the Wi-Fi: the location permission is only for Android to release the list of networks. The position is only read with the “Use location” switch on (it is off by default), for the sun-based theme and the weather, rounded to about 11 km, and it never leaves the phone or goes into the log.

What is stored on the phone?

  • A diagnostic log (on by default) to find bugs, with no position, streets, songs or caller (for WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal calls, only the app and the buttons). The bike’s Wi-Fi name is masked and the password is never recorded.
  • The dash’s Wi-Fi name and password and what the dash has already reported, encrypted with a key kept by Android.
  • If you turn them on: the rounded position, the last weather and the debug recordings (video and Android Auto sessions, which keep the map image and reveal where you rode; they are off by default).

Everything stays in the app’s internal folder and only leaves through Share (Settings → Debug). Logs delete themselves after 30 days (you choose 7, 14, 30 or never; 200 MB cap of logs and 200 MB of recordings). In Settings → Debug you can turn the log off, view it and delete logs and recordings.

Are the dash’s Wi-Fi and the picture encrypted?

The dash’s Wi-Fi is protected by the dash’s own password; the picture has no other encryption. REau doesn’t control that: it is how the dash works.

And what do Android Auto, Waze and Gemini do with my data?

That is out of REau’s reach and follows Google’s and Waze’s rules (your voice in Gemini, for example). Everything the app does with data is on the “REau and your data” screen, in the app itself.

15. How to report a problem

I found a bug or have a suggestion. How can I help?

What helps the author most, in this order:

  1. The log. The diagnostic log is already on. For more detail, turn on the “Detailed log” (a much larger file) in Settings → Debug before the ride. Afterwards, in Settings → Debug → Share → Logs, attach the file to an issue in the project’s repository (its address is on the app’s Credits screen). Look at the file before sending it: logs, recordings and photos may contain places you went, Wi-Fi names, phone numbers or contact names. Cut out what you don’t want public; when in doubt, describe it in words. On the road there is no way to see what is going on: what is not in the log doesn’t exist for the diagnosis.
  2. The rider’s mark. When something happens while riding, tap the Mark button in REau’s notification to mark the moment in the log.
  3. The context: the phone and the Android version; the REau version (footer of the home screen) and the edition (Lite or Full); the stretch (city or highway) and the approximate speed; what Waze was showing (many alerts, auto zoom, 2D or 3D).
  4. A photo or video of the dash, when possible, with the bike stopped (or filmed by someone or by a fixed camera).

Recordings (“Share → With recordings”) are large and reveal the route: only attach them if the author asks. The log is designed not to keep position, streets, songs or caller, but check before sending. Security flaw? Don’t use a public issue: use the repository’s private vulnerability report (Security tab, “Report a vulnerability”).
